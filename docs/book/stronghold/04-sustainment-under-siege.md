# Sustainment Under Siege

**Objective:** Detail physical sustainment systems for water, food, power, and medical support during isolation, including stockpiling ratios, rationing, and resupply-denial scenarios. This chapter is Chapter 4 of 10 in the Stronghold and Resilience series. Command and information continuity appear in Chapter 6.

## Water Systems and Reserves

Water constitutes the most time-critical consumable. Minimum planning figures specify 3 liters per person per day for drinking and cooking in temperate conditions, plus 7 to 10 liters for hygiene and medical use. Hot climates, heavy labor, and illness increase requirements by 30 to 100 percent.

Reliable strongholds combine at least two independent sources. Typical combinations include a drilled well plus rainwater harvesting, or a spring intake plus stored reserves. Each source requires separate treatment capability, such as ceramic filtration plus chlorination, or ultraviolet treatment plus boiling capacity during power loss.

Storage design separates daily-use tanks from sealed long-term reserves. Daily-use tanks permit rotation and quality monitoring. Sealed reserves remain untouched except during source failure. Materials include food-grade polyethylene, stainless steel, or lined concrete. Light exclusion and annual cleaning prevent algal growth and sediment accumulation.

Distribution planning addresses pumping energy, pipe vulnerability, and contamination control. Gravity feed from elevated tanks eliminates pumping during outages. Buried or protected piping reduces damage risk. Clear labeling prevents cross-connection between potable and non-potable lines.

## Food Storage and Nutritional Planning

Food planning balances caloric density, shelf life, preparation energy, and nutritional completeness. Target intake ranges from 2000 to 2400 kilocalories per adult per day under reduced activity, rising to 3000 or more during heavy labor or cold exposure.

Stockpiling ratios distinguish three tiers:

| Tier | Duration | Composition | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operational pantry | 14 to 30 days | Fresh, refrigerated, and daily staples | Normal consumption with rotation |
| Siege reserve | 90 to 180 days | Rice, beans, lentils, oats, oil, salt, canned goods | Core survival during isolation |
| Deep reserve | 180 to 365 days or more | Vacuum-sealed grains, freeze-dried meals, garden seeds | Extended holding and recovery |

Protein, fat, and micronutrients require explicit attention. Grains alone produce protein and niacin deficiencies within months. Stored legumes, oil, dried milk, multivitamins, and iodized salt close critical gaps. Sprouting of beans and cultivation of fast greens provide fresh vitamins when supply lines close.

Storage conditions control temperature, humidity, pests, and light. Hermetic containers with oxygen absorbers extend grain life beyond five years. Rotation schedules follow first-in-first-out discipline, with inventory logs recording quantities, expiration dates, and consumption rates. Pest management combines sealed containers, elevated pallets, traps, and quarantine of incoming goods.

## Power, Fuel, and Environmental Control

Power systems support water pumping, refrigeration, lighting, communications, medical devices, and tools. Strongholds typically combine grid connection where available with independent generation and storage.

Common architectures include:

- **Solar photovoltaic plus battery storage** for silent daytime generation and nighttime loads.
- **Diesel or propane generators** for high loads and battery recharging during overcast periods.
- **Wood or biomass heating** where forest resources permit, reducing liquid fuel dependence.
- **Manual alternatives** such as hand pumps, gravity ventilation, and passive solar design for critical functions during total power loss.

Fuel planning calculates daily consumption at essential-only load, normal load, and emergency load. Reserves store the larger of 90 days at essential load or the maximum plausible resupply interruption. Fuel stabilization, ventilated storage away from living quarters, and fire suppression accompany liquid fuel reserves.

Environmental control includes ventilation, heating, cooling, and waste management. Overcrowding without ventilation elevates carbon dioxide, moisture, and airborne disease transmission. Design standards specify air changes per hour, filter replacement intervals, and backup ventilation through operable windows or manual fans. Latrines, composting toilets, or sealed septic systems require separation from water sources by at least 30 meters and downhill placement.

## Medical Sustainment and Hygiene

Medical sustainment covers preventive hygiene, acute care, chronic disease management, and sanitation. Confinement increases transmission of respiratory and gastrointestinal illness. Prevention therefore outweighs treatment in resource efficiency.

Essential medical stores include wound care materials, splints, broad-spectrum antibiotics under qualified direction, oral rehydration salts, analgesics, antihistamines, chronic-disease medications for occupants with known conditions, and personal protective equipment. Quantities reflect occupant numbers, age distribution, and evacuation delay estimates.

Hygiene protocols specify handwashing stations, dish sterilization through boiling or chemical disinfection, laundry procedures, and isolation areas for symptomatic individuals. Waste segregation separates sharps, biological waste, and general refuse. Burial or controlled burning sites occupy downwind locations at safe distances from water intakes.

Training in basic first aid, cardiopulmonary resuscitation, hemorrhage control, and waterborne illness recognition precedes occupation. Medical records, stored redundantly in paper and sealed digital media, document allergies, prescriptions, and immunization status.

## Rationing and Resupply-Denial Scenarios

Rationing preserves reserves while maintaining function. Effective ration systems define trigger points, allocation rules, and monitoring methods before shortage occurs.

Trigger points link inventory levels to consumption stages. Example staging:

1. **Normal stage:** Full rations, routine rotation, above 75 percent of target reserve.
2. **Conservation stage:** Reduced variety, limited baths, enhanced garden output, at 50 to 75 percent.
3. **Restricted stage:** Measured caloric allocation, fuel for cooking and heating only, at 25 to 50 percent.
4. **Critical stage:** Minimum survival rations, medical priority allocation, below 25 percent.

Allocation follows transparent rules published to all occupants. Rules specify portions by age and labor category, cooking schedules to conserve fuel, and exemption criteria for illness or pregnancy. Opaque distribution generates distrust and hoarding.

Resupply-denial planning assumes zero external deliveries for the design duration. Scenarios include road blockade, infrastructure collapse, contamination of local sources, and hostile interdiction. Each scenario receives a response matrix identifying substitute sources, reduced-consumption procedures, and decision thresholds for breakout versus continued endurance, a choice analyzed further in Chapter 9. Regular inventory audits, consumption rehearsals, and sealed-reserve inspections validate sustainment calculations under realistic conditions.
