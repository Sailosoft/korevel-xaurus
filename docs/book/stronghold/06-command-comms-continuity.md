# Command, Comms and Continuity

**Objective:** Establish decision structures, redundant communications, records management, succession, and degraded-operations protocols for continuous control. This chapter is Chapter 6 of 10 in the Stronghold and Resilience series. Physical supplies appear in Chapter 4.

## Decision Structure and Authority

Command in a stronghold provides unity of effort under confinement and uncertainty. Structure remains simple, documented, and understood by all occupants. Typical roles include a coordinator with overall authority, deputies for security, logistics, medical affairs, and infrastructure, plus a recorder responsible for logs and archives.

Authority matrices define who may authorize gate opening, ration changes, medical triage priorities, power rationing, external communication, and evacuation. Low-consequence decisions delegate to functional leads. High-consequence decisions reserve to the coordinator with deputy concurrence. Emergency decisions permit immediate action to protect life, followed by prompt reporting and review.

Decision thresholds prevent paralysis. Examples include automatic water-conservation measures when reserves fall below 60 days, mandatory perimeter lockdown upon confirmed breach, and medical isolation upon diagnosis of transmissible illness. Pre-authorized thresholds reduce debate during crises and preserve legitimacy through prior agreement.

Meetings follow brief, regular rhythms. Daily coordination reviews inventory changes, perimeter incidents, maintenance status, and health reports. Weekly planning addresses resupply projections, training schedules, and repair priorities. Written minutes with action assignments and deadlines ensure follow-through.

## Redundant Communications

Communications connect observation posts, work teams, command points, and external contacts. Redundancy ensures that failure of one medium does not isolate critical nodes.

Primary systems often include handheld radios for mobile teams and a base station at the command point. Secondary systems include wired intercoms or field telephones between fixed posts, which function during radio interference or battery depletion. Tertiary systems include visual signals, runners, acoustic alarms, and message boards for total electronic failure.

Spectrum and power planning includes:

| System | Range | Power Need | Vulnerability |
|---|---|---|---|
| UHF handheld radios | 1 to 5 km | Rechargeable batteries | Interception, jamming |
| Wired intercom | Within facility | Low, central supply | Wire cuts |
| Cellular and internet | Variable | Grid plus backup | Infrastructure outage |
| Satellite messenger | Global | Battery plus solar | Subscription, sky view |
| Runner and visual signals | Line of sight | None | Slow, weather-limited |

Procedures cover call signs, authentication phrases, transmission brevity, scheduled check-ins, radio silence periods, and emission control during heightened threat. Spare batteries, solar chargers, antenna parts, and printed frequency plans store in shielded containers at two locations. Monthly communication exercises test each path under realistic load.

## Records, Documentation, and Knowledge Preservation

Records preserve situational awareness across shifts, rotations, and leadership changes. Core records include occupant rosters, inventory ledgers, perimeter logs, maintenance histories, medical files, financial accounts, and decision minutes.

Paper remains essential for degraded operations. Bound logbooks with numbered pages prevent undetected removal. Carbonless duplicates provide backup copies stored separately. Laminated checklists at posts guide consistent execution of gate procedures, alarm responses, and generator startup.

Digital records provide searchability and compact storage. Offline computers or tablets with encrypted drives hold scanned manuals, maps, schematics, and parts catalogs. Synchronization occurs on air-gapped media to prevent malware introduction. Update discipline requires that paper and digital versions carry identical revision dates and custodial signatures.

Archival protection addresses fire, water, and electromagnetic hazards. Fireproof safes house property titles, identification documents, and succession orders. Sealed containers elevate records above flood levels. Shielded enclosures protect backup drives containing irreplaceable data. Retention schedules prevent accumulation of obsolete paper while preserving evidence needed for accountability.

## Succession and Cross-Training

Continuity requires that loss or incapacitation of any individual does not halt essential functions. Succession orders list primary, alternate, and contingency assignees for each leadership and technical role. Orders remain posted in the command point and sealed in records storage.

Cross-training ensures depth. Each critical skill, including water treatment, generator maintenance, radio operation, first aid, inventory management, and gate control, requires at least two qualified operators plus one trainee. Qualification records document demonstrated competence rather than attendance alone. Rotation through secondary duties maintains proficiency and reveals single points of failure.

Temporary delegation procedures cover illness, rest periods, and external missions. Delegation specifies scope, duration, and reporting requirements. Return of authority follows formal handover with briefing on changes, incidents, and pending actions.

## Degraded-Operations Protocols

Degraded operations denote continued function with reduced personnel, power, communications, or supplies. Protocols define minimum staffing, essential loads, and simplified procedures for each degradation level.

Power degradation stages illustrate the approach:

1. **Full service:** All lighting, tools, and comfort loads permitted.
2. **Conservation:** Nonessential loads shed, cooking batched, laundry limited.
3. **Essential only:** Water pumping, refrigeration of perishables, medical devices, command communications, and minimal security lighting.
4. **Manual mode:** Hand pumping, passive ventilation, paper logging, runner messaging, and suspension of non-critical maintenance.

Personnel degradation protocols consolidate posts, extend but limit watch rotations, suspend training that consumes supplies, and prioritize sleep for critical operators. Communication degradation shifts from radio to wire to runner with corresponding changes in report frequency and message format.

Trigger criteria, authority for declaration, occupant notification methods, and recovery criteria for return to normal operations appear in written annexes. Practice of degraded modes, rather than theoretical familiarity alone, determines whether occupants can sustain coordination when infrastructure fails.
