# Chapter 8 — Logistics of Movement and Sustainment

**Series:** Strategic Advance & Tactical Operations Trainer (Chapter 8 of 10)

---

## 8.1 Amateurs Talk Tactics

An advance halts when its wheels stop turning. Logistics is not support to movement; it **is** movement, expressed in fuel, ammunition, water, food, spare parts, and medical capacity.

The three governing equations:

- **Range = fuel state ÷ consumption rate.** No plan outruns this number.
- **Combat endurance = loads carried ÷ expenditure rate.** Ammunition and casualty capacity set the deadline for every engagement during movement.
- **Tail-to-teeth ratio** — the fraction of the force handling supply. Too thin, the advance stalls; too thick, the advance has no teeth to hold what it takes.

A fourth equation governs the human: **movement rate = calories and water ÷ heat and load.** In warm environments, water consumption sets the daily bound before terrain does; a unit that plans its advance from the map and discovers the water problem at noon has planned a withdrawal for the afternoon.

## 8.2 Supply Along a Moving Line

- **Push versus pull:** during high-tempo exploitation, push supplies forward on predicted demand; in static or uncertain phases, pull on order. Exploitation outruns its logistics precisely because it switched from pull to push before it began.
- **Logistical halving:** like convoys, supply trains split at choke points and use alternate routes.
- **Forward arming and refuel points (FAORs):** pre-positioned, concealed, and mobile. A fuel point that takes an hour to displace is a gift to the enemy.
- **Repair and recovery:** a movement force that cannot recover its own mobility assets slows to the speed of its worst vehicle. Recovery precedes reinforcement.

Resupply methods follow tempo: **tailgate** (cargo moves to the user, slowest, most vulnerable), **service station** (users come to the supply, fastest on site, requires traffic control), and **delivery by launch or drone** for small critical items under contact. The choice is a trade between the user's tempo and the supply's exposure, and it is made per class of supply, not per convoy.

## 8.3 The Water, Medical, and Ammunition Triad

- **Water** sets the daily bound in hot environments; plan the bound from the water, not the map.
- **Medical:** casualty movement during advance is rearward traffic against forward movement — plan the evacuation lane before the operation, not at the first wound.
- **Ammunition:** expenditure in movement battles is spiky and unpredictable; distribute, don't concentrate, and keep the reserve allocation for the decisive fight unwed.

Casualty care under movement follows a fixed chain: self-aid, buddy-aid, litter to the trail element, vehicle to the collecting point, evacuation lane to definitive care. Every link that is not rehearsed becomes the reason the next casualty dies in the back of an armored vehicle that was never told to stop.

## 8.4 Maintenance and Materiel Readiness

Movement consumes machines. Standard maintenance is the cheapest combat power a force will ever buy: a scheduled service interval maintained during halts, operator-level checks before every departure, and recovery assets positioned where breakdowns actually occur — the grades, fords, and soft surfaces, not the motor pool. The force that skips maintenance to gain an hour of rest loses the vehicle and a day.

Materiel discipline extends to the load itself: everything carried must have a reason to move. The overweight column is slow, blind, and vulnerable; the trainer audits loads against the mission before every movement problem, because the enemy will do it for free on the route.

## 8.5 Seizing Logistics From the Situation

Operational movement can feed itself: captured fuel, local supply, and pre-positioned caches extend the bound. This is an option, never the plan — but the plan should identify where the option exists. Caches placed before movement are logistics; caches hoped for during movement are gambling. The difference is that the cache has coordinates, a guardian, and a report trigger.

## 8.6 The Logistics Battle Rhythm

Sustainment during movement is managed like tempo — on a rhythm, not on demand:

- **Status reports by class:** fuel, ammunition, water, food, and medical capacity reported at every phase line in the same format, so the commander reads the advance's remaining life as a single number.
- **Predicted demand, not ordered demand:** resupply trains are loaded for what the next twelve hours will consume, computed from expenditure rates and the scheme of maneuver; pull-based resupply always arrives after the fight it was ordered for.
- **The logistics update:** at each major phase transition, the commander re-answers one question — how long can the force keep moving and fighting at current expenditure? — and the answer sets the next bound's depth. Exploits die when this number goes unscheduled and unspoken.

## 8.7 Distributing and Protecting the Tail

The supply tail is the advance's most tempting target and its least armed element. Protect it with doctrine, not wishes:

- **Distribute stocks:** never concentrate a day of fuel or ammunition in one point; a dispersed loss is an inconvenience, a massed loss is an operation-ending event.
- **Displace on schedule:** supply points move on the clock and on warning, not only when contacted; a static logistics site is an intelligence report waiting to be filed.
- **Camouflage and dispersion at halts:** the tail's tracks, heat signatures, and traffic patterns are recon targets; the tail must practice the same signature discipline as the spear.
- **Route separation:** supply traffic uses alternate routes and dead intervals from tactical traffic wherever terrain allows, because one interdicted bridge should degrade either the fight or the feast, never both.

## 8.8 Trainer Objectives for This Chapter

- Compute maximum advance duration and depth for a given force from its organic supplies.
- Design a FAOR scheme supporting a 60-kilometer exploitation.
- Write a logistics branch: what changes when the primary resupply axis is interdicted at hour 36.
- Audit a movement load against mission requirements and justify every kilogram.
- Run the logistics battle rhythm for a two-day movement problem and report remaining life at each phase line.
